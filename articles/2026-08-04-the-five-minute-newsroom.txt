Every five minutes, a machine somewhere reaches out to two hundred newsrooms and asks the same question: what is happening now? The answer arrives in fragments — a headline from Reuters, a push alert from the AP, a regional paper filing on a story the nationals have not noticed yet.

That process is the spine of what you are reading. It is fast, it is tireless, and it does something no individual reporter can do, which is watch everything at once.

But speed is not comprehension. A wire that updates every five minutes can tell you that three outlets are covering the same event. It cannot tell you which one got it right.

## What aggregation is good at

Aggregation is very good at breadth. When a story breaks, the useful signal is often not the first report but the *shape* of the coverage — who is confirming, who is hedging, who is still silent.

- It surfaces stories that never reach the front page
- It shows you when outlets disagree, which is frequently the story itself
- It removes the accident of which single site you happened to open

That last point matters more than it sounds. Most people's picture of the world is shaped less by what is true than by which tab was already open.

> The value was never the headline. It was knowing how many people were saying it, and which ones had something to lose by being wrong.

## What it cannot do

An aggregator has no memory of last year. It cannot notice that a claim made confidently this morning contradicts one made by the same official in March. It cannot call anyone.

It also cannot decline to cover something. A feed has no editorial spine — if the inputs are noisy, the output is noisy, and the reader is left to do the sorting.

This is not an argument against automation. It is an argument for pairing it with something else.

## Where this column comes in

So this is the other half. Once a week, something here will be written rather than collected — a piece that takes one thread out of the five-minute churn and follows it somewhere the wire cannot go.

It will sometimes be wrong. That is the trade for it being *ours*, with a name attached and a correction policy behind it.

The wire will keep running underneath. It does not need us. But you might.
